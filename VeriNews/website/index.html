From one headline
to one clear verdict.

Every check follows the same six steps, written in C++ with our own data structures.

Step 1

Clean the headline

Lowercase, remove punctuation and common words like “the”, keep the keywords.

Step 2

Search the latest news

curl downloads Google News results for the keywords, plus a second search for fact-checks.

Step 3

Read and label each report

For every article we read the headline, publisher and time, and mark it trusted, unrated or fact-check.

Step 4

Store them

Reports go into a linked list, a hash table and a BST, and are saved to a file for offline use.

Step 5

Compare and rank

Similarity = 2 × common words ÷ total words. Insertion sort puts the best match first.

Step 6

Score, apply rules, explain

A score out of 100, nine rules that can override it, and the reason in plain words.

The data structures
behind every check.

Each one does one job. All of them are written by hand in C++.

Hash table

An array of 101 linked lists. A word's bucket is the sum of its letters' ASCII codes % 101, so finding the reports that contain a word means searching one short list.

Linked list

Stores every news record. It grows one node at a time as reports are downloaded.

Binary search tree

Orders news by date. Reading it left, node, right lists them from oldest to newest.

Stack

Recent checks, newest first: last in, first out.

Circular queue

Checks many headlines in the order they were typed: first in, first out.

Insertion sort and linear search

Sort ranks the matching reports, best first. Linear search finds a publisher's rating and avoids saving an article twice.

It gives an evidence-based credibility score. It does not prove that something is true.

It needs the internet and depends on Google News. News only minutes old may not be found yet.

Small local news, reported only by local papers, may get “needs verification”.

A fake that changes only a number (“25 killed” instead of “19”) can fool word matching.

Grounded in
published research.

We read these papers before designing VeriNews, and each one shaped a part of it.

Ahmed et al., 2021

Detecting Fake News using Machine Learning: A Systematic Literature Review

Reviews 26 studies and the standard steps: remove stop words, stem, split, train, measure.

We use the same text cleaning: lowercase, stop words, plural removal.

Fedushko, Syerov and Kryvinska, 2024

AntiFake System: Machine Learning-Based System for Verification of Fake News

Classifies articles from their text and suggests searching for known false statements.

Our fact-check search and stored fake stories follow that suggestion.

Tanaja et al., 2025

Integrating FakeBERT Classification, Style Analysis, and Credibility Verification

Compares a claim with related news scraped from trusted outlets.

Our live search compares each headline with today's reports from trusted publishers.
